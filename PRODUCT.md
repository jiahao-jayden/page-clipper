# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Heavy knowledge-base users who clip web pages into a durable personal or team knowledge system. The current implementation writes to Obsidian, but the product direction is broader: a focused clipping tool for getting web content into a knowledge base with minimal cleanup.

## Product Purpose

Obsidian Wiki Clipper extracts the active page, normalizes it into readable Markdown, lets the user review or annotate it, and saves it into a knowledge-base destination. Success means clipping feels quick, trustworthy, and low-friction: the user can understand what will be saved, make a small adjustment when needed, and move on.

## Brand Personality

Calm, precise, restrained. The interface should feel like a reliable writing and knowledge tool: quiet enough to stay out of the way, exact enough to earn trust, and compact enough for repeated daily use.

## Anti-references

This should not look like a SaaS marketing surface with gradients, inflated cards, or decorative motion. It should not over-mimic Obsidian with a heavy plugin aesthetic or unnecessary darkness. It should also avoid terminal or hacker styling that makes the clipping workflow feel more technical than it is.

## Design Principles

1. Preserve the user's flow: every interaction should reduce the distance between page, preview, and saved knowledge.
2. Make extraction trustworthy: show enough source, status, and preview context for the user to know what is being saved.
3. Stay visually quiet: use restraint, density, and familiar product patterns instead of decorative effects.
4. Keep destination flexibility in mind: Obsidian is the first target, but the design should not hard-code the product identity around Obsidian alone.
5. Prefer precise feedback over ceremony: loading, errors, copying, and saving should be immediate and clearly worded.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA for contrast and interaction states. The UI should be keyboard-usable, expose clear focus states, avoid conveying status by color alone, and respect reduced-motion preferences.
